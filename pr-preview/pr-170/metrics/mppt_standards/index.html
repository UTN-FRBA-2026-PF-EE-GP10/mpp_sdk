    International Standards for MPPT Evaluation¶

    Summary of the three main normative frameworks for evaluating MPPT algorithms, based on the comparative analysis "Comparación Crítica de Normas MPPT en Sistemas Fotovoltaicos" (Critical Comparison of MPPT Standards in Photovoltaic Systems) (author and year to be added).

    1. EN 50530:2018¶

    Scope: evaluates the inverter only (not the complete system). It is the strictest standard for characterising the core of an MPPT algorithm in the laboratory.

    Metrics¶

    Static efficiency:

    \[\eta_{stat} = \frac{P_{dc,measured}}{P_{mpp,simulator}}\]

    Measured in steady state at 7 power levels (5 %, 10 %, 20 %, 30 %, 50 %, 75 %, 100 % of nominal) and multiple voltage levels. The goal is to map how the algorithm behaves at different operating points of the panel.

    Dynamic efficiency:

    \[\eta_{dyn} = \frac{\int P_{dc}\,dt}{\int P_{mpp}\,dt} \times 100\%\]

    Measured during synthetic irradiance ramps with defined slopes between 0.5 and 100 W/(m²·s). These ramps represent transitions such as a passing cloud. The profiles are defined in Annex B of the standard.

    Weighted efficiency (CEC/EUR):

    To obtain a single number representative of annual performance, the standard weights η_stat at different load levels with standardised factors:

    \[\eta_{CEC} = \sum a_i \cdot \eta_i\]
    Power level CEC weight EUR weight
    5 % - 0.03
    10 % 0.04 0.06
    20 % 0.05 0.13
    30 % 0.12 0.10
    50 % 0.21 0.48
    75 % 0.53 0.20
    100 % 0.05 -

    The CEC weights reflect the climate of California (lots of sun → high weight at 75 %); the EUR weights reflect central Europe (more hours at medium power → high weight at 50 %).

    EN 50530: test requirements¶

    Parameter Value
    Sampling frequency ≥ 10 Hz
    Maximum uncertainty ≤ 0.5 %
    Inter-lab reproducibility CV < 0.25 %
    Test temperature T = 25 °C fixed (static)
    Dynamic profiles Synthetic ramps (Annex B)

    EN 50530: limitations¶

    • Focuses exclusively on the inverter → ignores losses from mismatch between modules, partial shading or multi-MPPT configurations.
    • Does not address P-V curves with multiple local maxima (partial shading).
    • Temperature fixed at 25 °C → does not capture degradation from heating.
    • Does not link the efficiency metrics to economic indicators (LCOE).

    2. IEC 62816:2014¶

    Scope: evaluates the complete system (modules + inverter). It is the standard with the best balance between technical rigour and representativeness of real conditions.

    Main metric: εTL (Tracking Loss)¶

    \[\varepsilon_{TL} = \frac{E_{mpp} - E_{dc}}{E_{mpp}} \times 100\%\]

    Quantifies the energy loss caused by imperfections in tracking the MPP. It is the complement of the energy efficiency:

    \[\varepsilon_{TL} = (1 - \eta_{energy}) \times 100\%\]

    The standard classifies algorithms into classes according to εTL (number of classes to be checked against the standard):

    Class εTL
    A < 1 %
    B 1 – 2 %
    C 2 – 5 %
    D 5 – 10 %
    E > 10 %

    Main advantage: εTL translates directly into energy not generated (kWh/year) and therefore into economic impact ($/year). If a 1 kW system with a 20 % capacity factor has εTL = 2 %, it loses ~3.5 kWh/year - scaled to MW, the differences are significant.

    Methodological approach¶

    Unlike EN 50530, IEC 62816 allows:

    • Using real time series recorded in situ (not only synthetic ramps).
    • Evaluating the complete system including modules, cabling and inverter.
    • Capturing the effect of partial shading (generic reference).

    IEC 62816: test requirements¶

    Parameter Value
    Sampling frequency ≥ 1 Hz
    Maximum uncertainty ≤ 1 %
    Inter-lab reproducibility CV < 0.40 %
    Test temperature Real T, measured
    Minimum duration Until covering 90 % of annual variability

    IEC 62816: limitations¶

    • Lower time resolution (≥ 1 Hz vs. ≥ 10 Hz for EN 50530) → can mask ultra-fast transients.
    • Makes it hard to tell fast-response algorithms apart in scenarios with very abrupt changes.

    3. ASTM E2848¶

    Scope: evaluates the complete plant (large-scale photovoltaic system). It prioritises simplicity and low implementation cost.

    Metric: regression model¶

    Regression of power on irradiance, temperature and wind speed (exact form: see the standard). The coefficients are determined statistically from historical field data.

    It does not define a tracking efficiency metric per se - it infers the loss as the difference between the power predicted by the model and the measured power.

    ASTM E2848: test requirements¶

    Parameter Value
    Sampling frequency 1/60 Hz (1-min averages)
    Maximum uncertainty Not set (σ ≈ 1.1 %)
    Inter-lab reproducibility Not set
    Minimum duration 3 – 30 field days

    ASTM E2848: limitations¶

    • 1-minute time resolution → completely smooths out the transients of the MPPT algorithm.
    • High dispersion of results (σ ≈ 1.1 %) → makes inter-laboratory comparison difficult.
    • Unsuitable for comparing individual MPPT algorithms - its purpose is plant-level performance characterisation for annual projections.

    Main gap (Type I)¶

    None of the three standards links the performance metrics (η_dyn, εTL) to economic indicators such as LCOE. According to the comparison paper, this disconnect:

    • Weakens the commercial case for adopting more advanced algorithms.
    • Leaves the economic value of MPPT efficiency improvements unquantified.
    • The difference of up to 0.7 % absolute in the efficiency reported for the same algorithm (depending on the standard) can change the ranking in tenders and has a direct economic impact.

    Partial shading gap¶

    No standard provides a standardised procedure for evaluating behaviour on multi-peak P-V curves (partial shading). This is precisely what our cyclic harness (compare_cyclic.py) does: it generates partial-shading profiles with multiple local maxima and evaluates the escape capability of the global algorithms.

    5. Applicability to our project¶

    Aspect EN 50530 / IEC 62816 Our Implementation & Bench Scenarios
    Sampling frequency ≥ 10 Hz (EN 50530) / ≥ 1 Hz (IEC 62816) 1 kHz (sim) / ~22 Hz (~46 ms, hardware HIL)
    Dynamic profiles Synthetic ramps Simulation: cyclic profile with ramps. Hardware: Scenario 3 (repeatable stepped dimmer profile)
    Partial shading Not covered (EN 50530) / Generic (IEC) Scenario 2: PvString with bypass diodes, 2 panels @ 45° tilt (multi-peak P-V curve)
    Dynamic efficiency η_dyn energy_efficiency() ≡ η_dyn (scored against pre-calibrated dimmer plateau sweeps)
    Tracking loss εTL = 1 - η_dyn Calculated as 1 - η_E on our dimmer profile
    Test T 25 °C fixed Simulation: configurable. Hardware: panel temperature not measured in the first bench session

    Note

    We are evaluating the isolated MPPT algorithm running on a SEPIC converter, not a complete commercial grid-tied inverter. By implementing Scenario 3 (repeatable stepped dimmer profile), we apply the core principles of dynamic tracking characterization from EN 50530 and IEC 62816 directly to our physical bench without requiring an industrial solar simulator.
