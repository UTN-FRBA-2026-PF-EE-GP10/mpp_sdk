    SEPIC settling time¶

    Why the step response was slow, what fixed it, and the measured result.

    Cause¶

    • A step on the duty cycle took about 100 ms to settle at a 50 kHz switching frequency. That is about 5000 switching cycles (20 us each).
    • The main cause was the \(1000\,\mu\text{F}\) input capacitor in parallel with the panel. The panel has limited current, so it charges and discharges that capacitor slowly.
    • You cannot remove the input capacitor. Without it the panel sees the raw 50 kHz ripple and the ADC readings are distorted. A value of \(22 - 100\,\mu\text{F}\) is enough for the ripple.

    Decision¶

    Four changes, in this order:

    1. Smaller capacitor. Replace the \(1000\,\mu\text{F}\) capacitor with a \(22\,\mu\text{F}\) low-ESR part. The RC time constant falls by about 5 times. The worst hardware lag falls from 68.4 ms to about 15 ms.
    2. 20 ms mains notch. The lamps of the solar simulator produce a 100 Hz ripple of \(\pm 30 - 50\,\text{mV}\). A burst of 25 samples over 1.25 ms (50 us spacing) caught a random phase of it. This inverted the \(\Delta V\) slope in fine MPPT steps (0.2 % to 0.5 %) and the voltage drifted toward \(V_\text{oc}\). The firmware now spaces the 25 samples by 800 us (Timer::after_micros(800)). The window is exactly 20 ms: 1 cycle of 50 Hz and 2 cycles of 100 Hz. The sine ripple integrates to zero and the steady-state ripple falls to 0.0 mV pp.
    3. Outlier rejection and flush. The firmware takes a 25-sample trimmed mean (drops the 4 lowest and 4 highest, averages the middle 17). On a duty change command the Pico clears its buffers, waits for the hardware transition, computes the settled value and holds that one measurement. It does not stream raw readings.
    4. Driver and step size.
    5. SpiMcuSource has a raw mode (settling_time_s = 0.0). The step response tool scripts/sepic_step_response.py uses it to record transients every 3 ms.
    6. For closed-loop tracking, settling_time_s is 0.05 (50 ms). This covers the filter and the hardware.
    7. With \(\Delta D = 0.002\) (0.2 %), the algorithms converge to \(V_\text{mpp} \approx 14.30\,\text{V}\) (error under 0.6 % of the sweep peak) with an oscillation span of about 1.0 V.

    An adaptive P&O step size is a separate software option. It reduces the number of steps and does not change the settling time of one step.

    Measured result¶

    Duty steps from 0.40 to 0.70 into a \(10\,\Omega\) load. Settling times in ms:

    Duty step \(V_\text{in}\) mean \(V_\text{out}\) \(1000\,\mu\text{F}\), no filter \(22\,\mu\text{F}\), 1.25 ms burst \(22\,\mu\text{F}\) + 20 ms notch
    0.40 to 0.45 15.50 V 11.57 to 13.52 V 7.3 14.1 41.1
    0.45 to 0.50 13.74 V 13.52 to 14.53 V 23.8 5.5 45.6
    0.50 to 0.55 9.58 V 14.53 to 12.15 V 68.4 14.6 42.0
    0.55 to 0.60 6.55 V 12.15 to 10.05 V 55.3 14.6 44.5
    0.60 to 0.65 4.38 V 10.05 to 8.12 V 41.7 0.0 (artifact) 42.1
    0.65 to 0.70 2.87 V 8.12 to 6.44 V 28.2 0.0 (artifact) 42.0
    • The \(V_\text{out}\) and \(V_\text{in}\) ripple is 0.0 mV pp in the last column.
    • The 0.0 ms values are artifacts. The ripple phase hid the transition.
    • The settling time with the notch is uniform: 41.1 to 45.6 ms, mean 42.9 ms. It is the sum of two parts:
    • power stage: about 22.9 ms (L-C-L tank and coupling capacitor, within the \(\pm 2\%\) band, with the \(22\,\mu\text{F}\) capacitor),
    • notch window: 20.0 ms (25 samples x 800 us).
    • \(V_\text{out}\) peaks at 14.53 V (\(P \approx 21.1\,\text{W}\) into \(10\,\Omega\)) at \(D = 0.50\) (\(V_\text{in} \approx 13.74\,\text{V}\)). This matches the panel MPP.

    Analog divider range: Full or Low¶

    Low (8.5x) gives 2.76 times finer ADC voltage resolution than Full (23.5x). The quantization noise is lower.

    Metric Full (235 k / 10 k) Low (85 k / 10 k)
    Divider ratio 23.5x 8.5x
    Full scale about 75.6 V about 27.3 V
    1 LSB about 23.5 mV about 8.5 mV
    Ripple, step 0.40 to 0.45 94.0 mV pp (about 4 LSB) 43.0 mV pp (about 5 LSB)
    Ripple, step 0.45 to 0.50 212.0 mV pp 51.0 mV pp
    Ripple, step 0.55 to 0.60 517.0 mV pp 77.0 mV pp

    For setups with \(V_\text{oc} \le 27.3\,\text{V}\), Low gives the best gradient sensitivity for fine-step MPPT.
